# Wanigan Interface Review

    Three questions, answered from evidence rather than taste: why T3 Code and BridgeMind read as cleaner than Wanigan and what that difference is actually made of; what a cleaner, more interactive Wanigan looks like as a verified, prioritised roadmap; and how the three products handle context, memory and skills across models, where Wanigan is behind and where it is quietly ahead.

    01 Part one · the visual gap

    
## Why T3 Code and BridgeMind read as cleaner, and what that is made of

    Three critics, one per angle, diagnosed the gap from the screenshots and both codebases. Twenty of their causes and recommendations then went through three adversarial lenses each before the quota ran out. What follows keeps the verifiers' corrections, including two places where the critics' explanation was wrong even though the symptom was real.

  
  
## The straight answer

The visual gap is made of five things, in this order of weight. First, prose. On first paint Wanigan explains itself: 412 words on Plugins, 361 on Settings, 298 on Schedules, 287 on Learning, 247 on Scout, 213 on Control, run at roughly 180 characters per line. T3 Code's empty screen carries 16 words, its new-thread screen 17, and its working screen zero words of interface explanation because every visible sentence is agent output. BridgeMind's Agent screen carries about 70 words, all of them labels. Second, chrome. Wanigan spends 100 CSS pixels on a two-row header (a 52px title bar plus a 48px rail of 13 text tabs) on every view, and Learning stacks three more bands under it, so its first control lands about 48% of the way down a 900px window. T3 spends one 52px bar; BridgeMind's window spends about 35px. Third, one accent doing every job. Coral marks the New session button, the selected segment, the Inbox count pill, inline code, the hero digit on Control, the example rule, the focus ring and the terminal cursor at once, so nothing on screen is the thing to click. T3 spends its single blue on button, switch, send and focus and gives selection a neutral fill. Fourth, boxes. Every card, tile, input and note gets the same 1px line, cards nest three deep with identical borders, and the header and nav share the card fill, so chrome and content read as one plane of outlined rectangles. Fifth, a second typeface as a label convention: the DIN Condensed stencil eyebrow appears at 235 sites, nine of them on the Control screen alone, above fields, table heads and palette groups, while both competitors run one family and separate levels with weight and one or two greys.

The structural half is information architecture. Wanigan has 15 views, 13 of them on the rail, one per mechanism: four execution mechanisms (Sessions, Runs, Batches, Schedules) each get a top-level destination, Insights and Usage are two views over spend, Skills, Context and Plugins are three views over what the CLI loads, Learning and Scout are two inboxes, and the running session is shown on three surfaces at once (rail, Fleet, the ATTENTION strip). Counting the four title-bar controls and the strip, the shell presents 18 nouns before any content. T3 presents four: project, thread, search, settings. Every one of Wanigan's rail tabs was added between 27 August and 4 September and none was ever removed. Explainers never collapse and sit above the page instead of at the point of choice; forms are embedded in empty states so fields appear that cannot yet be completed; launching a session is a nine-section scrolling modal where both competitors make the composer the launch form; and at 960px the rail scrolls horizontally under a 5px scrollbar with Runs and Settings off-screen.

The honest part is scope, process and staging. T3 buys its primitives: 47 shadcn-style components over base-ui, Lucide icons, class-variance-authority variants, 211 design tokens including control height, row insets and top-bar height, and 168 of its 249 feature files import from that layer. Wanigan's primitive layer is five functions in one file, styled with 16 inline objects; the views carry 1,246 inline style objects (Settings 269, Batches 205, Sessions 128) and 68 tokens that stop at colour, type and spacing. T3 enforces its system with a custom lint rule, 314 web tests and a before/after-screenshot rule for UI changes; Wanigan's test suite never renders the renderer and the repository contains no screenshot at all. And you are comparing Wanigan's unseeded first-run state with curated, populated marketing: T3's hero is a real 2880×1800 capture of a settled thread beside a diff, and BridgeMind's homepage "app" is a live HTML re-creation with a scripted transcript and a wireframe browser pane, not a screenshot of the shipping binary. Their Chat mode is never rendered, and rail rows the demo shows were removed in their own v0.1.20 and v0.1.25 releases. None of that makes the gap imaginary. It does mean part of what you reacted to is staging.

## Verified causes

Each cause below was checked by three independent verifiers against the screenshots and code. Where a verifier corrected the critic, the correction is what appears here.

Prose is the content. On every one of the eight measured non-Sessions views the first paint is Wanigan explaining itself. The word counts above are reproduced exactly from the screenshots; the "How Control works" card still renders after a goal exists, so it is undismissable onboarding rather than first-run help, and Plugins repeats the same 40-word "Nothing on disk records…" paragraph under each of nine cards. The verifiers split this into two kinds of text and disagreed with the critics about one of them. Teaching prose (mechanism explanations, guides, glossary paragraphs) can shrink to one line and move behind a disclosure. State prose must not: disabled-button reasons, "? enabled state not read", "Nothing recorded in this window", tilde and est. marks and the gate-check lines are the honesty the values require on first paint. Commit 562c2e4 also records that a collapsed Learning drawer was removed because it hid the explanation from the person who needed it, so any collapse must open on first visit and close only when the operator closes it, never because data arrived.

[image]Wanigan Learning: 287 words of explanation on first paint before any row of data; the pipeline strip is the only glance-able element.
[image]Wanigan Settings: 361 words; a "How settings work" card, a "How changes apply" callout and four paragraphs precede the first field.
[image]Wanigan Control: 213 words; the quick-start occupies the fold while the one real docket sits below it.

Chrome stacks vertically. The header measures 100 CSS pixels in every capture (index.css sets the title bar to 52 and the rail to 48). Sessions adds a 33px attention strip and, once a session exists, a 38px tab strip and a 28px status bar; Learning adds its head paragraph and scope row, a five-station pipeline spine and a tab row, so its first control sits near 400 CSS pixels. The two-row header is a documented decision made for tablet widths, and the 960px capture shows that decision failing its own rationale: the rail clips at Git with Runs and Settings hidden. The verifiers rejected the critics' framing that the competitors' 35 to 52px is a design achievement; it is the price of exposing three or four destinations. The corrected cause is that Wanigan pays 100px to show 13 destinations and then lets three views add explanatory bands of their own.

One coral accent does every job. Verified by pixel sampling: on the Learning capture the Teach button, the selected 30-day segment and the Inbox count pill are all coral or coral-soft; on Control, so are the hero zero, inline code, the example rule and the selected docket's border. Wanigan's own stylesheet states the rule the build violates: "Colour is an event in this system and happens twice: the plate-split underline and the lamp in the mark." The verifiers added a constraint the critics missed: the dark palette has only about 1.12:1 between the sunk and soft surfaces, so selection cannot simply move to the hover fill or it disappears. A distinct selected-fill step of about 1.4:1 has to exist first.

Hierarchy is carried by borders, not surfaces, but not for the reason the critics gave. The symptom holds: surface steps are 1.06, 1.12 and 1.19:1 while the line is 1.65 to 1.75:1, the title bar, nav and cards all sample as the same colour, and Scout nests box inside box inside box. The mechanism was wrong. Measured from the captures, BridgeMind's window-to-card step is 1.11:1, identical to Wanigan's, and T3's dark theme explicitly rejects fills in favour of faint borders. What differs is count and nesting: BridgeMind draws two levels with one outline each and lets the ground show around inset cards; T3 uses white at 6% for dividers. So the fix is to soften and reduce lines rather than to invent a tonal ladder Wanigan's competitors do not actually use.

Two typographic voices. Verified: 235 stencil label sites, nine eyebrows on one Control screen, nine on the New-session dialog, three over an empty Usage page. Commit 5054174 is the load-bearing decision the critics did not answer: DIN Condensed was chosen as the app's voice, "the face on machinery plates and crate stencils", to be used twice, on the nameplate and on small-caps labels. The verifiers kept the face and narrowed the second use: one eyebrow per surface or card head keeps the voice; form-field labels, table heads, palette group labels and stat captions are controls, not sections, and belong in the system face.

No shared page frame. Left edges land at 26, 32, 19.5 and 3.5 CSS pixels across Fleet, Control, Plugins and Usage; titles are 19, 20 and 28px. The codebase already calls this drift a bug: the breakpoint-ladder comment asks contributors to "reach for one before inventing a thirteenth width", and control.css records that keeping Control off the shared pane caused a visible padding mismatch. Usage is the extreme case: its root class has no rule in any stylesheet, so it renders with no gutter and a browser-default heading.

[image]Usage renders flush to the window edge: the only view without the shared pane gutter, and it prints "Nothing recorded" while still reading.
[image]At 960px the rail overflows: Runs and Settings are off-screen with a 5px scrollbar as the only affordance.

Iconography drifts. No icon library; the 13 inline SVGs are all charts; the theme glyph is set at the lead size in accent and outweighs its label. The verifiers refuted the blanket fix. Status glyphs are text characters on purpose in four places in the code, because a shape survives greyscale and reads to assistive technology as text. The corrected recommendation separates two classes: status marks stay as characters paired with words but get one rule that fixes their optical box and weight; affordance icons (disclosure chevrons, plus, theme, panel toggle, external link, close) move to a small inline-SVG set, always beside a word.

Type and radius scales are declared but not held. 162 literal font-size declarations in 16 sizes beside 178 token uses; 20 distinct radii beside three tokens; the shortcut sheet asks for a radius token that was never defined and renders square-cornered. T3 leaks the same way on font sizes, so this is not what makes T3 look cleaner; it is a place where Wanigan can be strictly better cheaply.

Empty states are paragraphs in a full-width box. True for Fleet (67 words in a hairline card over 58% blank viewport) and Insights (about 130 words plus a nested budget form). The critics overreached on Batches and Usage: Batches already renders three words and one button, and Usage's failure is the opposite, no frame at all. Sessions already has the right composition, so the fix is to make the outliers match Wanigan's own best view rather than import T3's.

Nothing enforces the system. No lint configuration of any kind, no UI tests, no screenshot rule, no images in the repository; the test suite exercises the main process only. T3's polish is procedural: a custom oxlint rule forbids native title tooltips in favour of the Tooltip primitive, and its contributing guide requires before-and-after images for any UI change. This cause was not adversarially verified because the verification pass ran out of quota, but every fact in it was reproduced from the two repositories.

The demo is staged. BridgeMind's homepage window says "Not a screenshot, the window is live." It is a re-creation: scripted transcript, grey wireframe blocks where the browser page would be, and a homepage platform claim that contradicts its own docs page. Its Draft row shows a grey check on an action still awaiting approval, identical to completed rows. Wanigan's captures are unseeded views with 60 to 75% blank canvas. This cause also awaits adversarial verification, but the evidence is the captures themselves.

## What to adopt, in order

Ranked by impact over effort. Each item is the verifiers' revised form, and each names the value or recorded decision it respects.

Split prose into state and teaching, and move only the teaching. Add two small components: a one-sentence hint capped at 64 characters wide, and a disclosure that is open on first visit and remembered per view once the operator closes it, never auto-closed by data. Apply to Control's guide card, Learning's four-stages card and glossary paragraphs, Settings' two explainer cards and four-paragraph key text, Scout's safety card and the repeated Plugins disclaimer, which should render once at the section head. Leave every disabled-reason, estimate mark and gate-check line where it is. Effort small to medium. Respects "say the true thing" and the 562c2e4 decision.
Route every document view through the existing pane frame. Fleet, Control, Insights, Usage, Learning, Scout, Plugins, Schedules, Batches, Runs, Settings, Skills and Context take the shared gutter, 20px title and head row; Control keeps its centred width as a modifier; Sessions and Git stay pane layouts. Fix Usage's undefined root class in the same change. Effort small. Completes the breakpoint-ladder decision.
Hold the scales. Define the missing large radius (12px), a 2px extra-small radius and a 10px tiny type step, then map every literal in the stylesheets onto the tokens. Effort small. No decision affected.
Reserve coral for act-now and the three recorded events. Primary buttons, New session, the palette's primary row and send controls keep the accent, as do the plate-split active tab, the plate-red selection edge and the focus ring. Everything else drops to neutral or to the type scale, in this order of certainty: the solid coral model and effort chips in the New-session dialog, Control's hero digit and inline code, then segments, chips and count pills. Add a distinct selected-fill step before moving selection off the accent. Effort medium. Respects "colour is an event that happens twice".
Quiet the "needs key" mark. Transparent fill, hairline border, faint text, glyph and word unchanged; full-colour warnings stay on the Batches header and in Settings where the action is. Effort small. Completes the recorded reason the mark moved to Batches in the first place: it "read as Wanigan is not set up, from every screen".
Make Fleet and Insights use the Sessions empty state. Centred title, one true sentence, one button, no card. Keep the four states distinct (loading is not empty, empty is not zero-results, a failed read is neither), which Insights, Batches and Learning each document. Effort small. Respects the no-demo-rows rule.
Keep the stencil where the voice lives. Nameplate, the ATTENTION lead word and one eyebrow per surface or card head. Demote field labels, table heads, palette group labels and stat captions to the system face at 12px 500, sentence case. Effort small once the label class is split in two. Respects commit 5054174.
Soften and reduce lines instead of inventing a tonal ladder. Card and sunk borders move to the soft line token; the full line is reserved for inputs and table dividers; the third nesting level loses its border; header and nav get a fill distinct from cards; widen the ground-to-card step modestly while keeping every named text colour above 4.5:1 on the sunk surface. Effort medium. Respects the contrast contract.
Two classes of glyph. One rule for status characters (fixed 1em box, inherited weight, tabular width); a small inline-SVG set for affordance icons, drawn at 16px with a 1.75 stroke in the dim text colour, always beside a word. Effort medium. Respects glyph-plus-word-before-colour.
Give controls a hairline bevel. A 1px inset highlight and a soft under-shadow on buttons, fields and the title-bar controls, with the border dropped to about 60% alpha. Effort small. No motion, no compositor cost near the terminal.
Cut the chrome height. Two separable fixes: fold the 13 destinations out of the horizontal axis (the sidebar restructure in Part 2), and cut the view-head prose on Learning, Control and Plugins. Effort large for the first, small for the second.

## What not to adopt

A chat transcript replacing the terminal. The rule against simulating an agent interaction is about execution, and the Composer and Timeline comments already fix the contract: the terminal is authoritative, the hook bus is the record. Render recorded evidence beside the PTY; never in place of it.
BridgeMind's title-bar mode switch. Three whole-app modes multiply concepts rather than reduce them, the evidence is a marketing embed, and BridgeMind itself is converging on T3's one-list model: its v0.1.25 removed the Thread pane from Code mode and rebuilt Chat, in its own words, "the way T3 Code works".
Credits, PRO badge and account identity in the rail footer. No cloud tier, ever. The honest analogue, live per-account limits, already exists as the Usage view.
T3's Full-access default for new threads. Wanigan's per-project trust default is the operator-as-constraint answer and must survive any composer-first launch.
The "What should we work on?" register. Adopt the shape (one question, the noun as a control), not the anthropomorphic "we".
Zero explanation anywhere. Mechanism sentences like "Fleet watches sessions that already exist; it does not start them" are a say-the-true-thing asset. Relocate behind a disclosure; do not delete. BridgeMind later had to add exactly this text at its failure points.
Identical marks for pending and done. BridgeMind gives an awaiting-approval row the same grey check as a completed one, and colours additions and deletions both green. Those are truth problems Wanigan is ahead on.
Settlement on a timer. Adopt the collapsed "Settled (n)" shelf; not T3's three-day auto-settle. What leaves the active list is the operator's decision.
A Tailwind plus shadcn rewrite. T3's gain comes from tokens and one component layer, not the tool: it still has about 250 arbitrary pixel sizes, an undefined heading utility and 900-character class strings.
Glass, grain, backdrop blur, pulsing dots, spinners and shimmer. Compositor cost beside a live xterm, which the motion contract forbids, and nothing communicated.
Glyph-only identity, sub-11px metadata and 40 to 60% opacity secondary text. T3's muted foreground at 40 to 65% and BridgeMind's 9 to 10px metadata at roughly 3.6:1 fail the floor Wanigan commits to. Steal the discipline, not the contrast.
Swapping the warm palette for neutral zinc. The hue is the identity and the light theme is genuinely good. The gap is surface steps, accent discipline and prose, none of which requires a neutral base.
Collapsing the 15 views into a thread list. Fleet, Control, Batches, Learning, Scout and Runs are different jobs by design and are where Wanigan is ahead. Reduce chrome, not scope.

## How to make Wanigan cleaner and more interactive

## What the evidence says

The shell. Thirteen tabs plus three chord-only views, a title bar with four controls of which two are redundant (the Headless-runs button navigates to the Runs tab 48px below it; the Views button opens the same palette as the ⌘K it prints), and a rail whose left-to-right order no longer matches the ⌘1 to ⌘9 map because Usage and Scout were inserted between digits. The shortcut sheet promises to change with the code and did not: it lacks ⌘⇧I and ⌘⇧U and describes the palette as smaller than it is. Session tabs print ⌘1 to ⌘9 hints that the shell consumes in the capture phase, so those bindings can never fire. Every in-view dialog paints under the header because the body's view-transition name creates a stacking context, so the New-session dialog opens with its heading hidden and the nav still clickable while aria-modal is true. Palette rows shrink-wrap, so the shortcut column floats mid-row. At 960px the rail scrolls under a 5px scrollbar.

The live-work surface. A running session stacks the attention strip, the tab bar, a run-config bar, an always-mounted attachment strip with a three-line empty paragraph, the composer and the status bar before the terminal gets a row. Two unnamed sessions in one project render identical tabs distinguished only by a 6px colour dot; unread is a count of output chunks with no legible unit. Clicking a Timeline file row launches an external editor while the Code pane sits beside it unused. The terminal-to-panel split is fixed. The only advertised way out of the terminal by keyboard is "click outside it first". The New-session dialog scrolls at 1440×900 with Start below the fold.

The dashboards. Usage roots on a class no stylesheet defines, prints an empty state while still reading, and paints its meter track the same colour as the card it sits in, with a fallback hex because its danger token was never defined. Fleet's four stat tiles are inert while the chip row two lines below filters by exactly what they count; its empty state tells you to press ⌘T in a chip but renders no button, though the CSS for that button exists. Control renders its quick-start guide above a docket that already exists, and the selected goal's task graph sits below the fold; the graph never reads its own dependency data, so a blocked node cannot say what blocks it. Insights promises "click to open" on bars that are wired to nothing.

The knowledge and management surfaces. Learning encodes its pipeline three times above the fold (spine counts, a four-stages card, tab badges) and spends about 272 CSS pixels of fixed chrome before the scroll well; the single pending proposal's Approve and Reject buttons sit below the fold behind two drawers and a provider-targets row, and the gate checks that explain the automated decision load only on click. Settings puts a hero paragraph, an explainer aside, a rail intro and a per-tab explainer before the first control, which lands near 800 CSS pixels down. A saved schedule cannot be edited; the copy says delete it and create it again, and Delete fires on one click. Git commit rows are divs with onClick and no keyboard path; each file row nests an interactive span inside a button. Plugins repeats a 40-word disclaimer under nine cards. Headless squash-merge merges a worktree on one click while Git confirms the same act; account rename uses window.prompt and a Batches error uses alert.

The foundations. Six references to tokens that do not exist. Seventeen literal transition durations bypass the motion tokens, so the operator's motion-off setting is not honoured everywhere. About twenty chip families and eleven status-pill families sit beside the one Pill in bits.tsx, four of them without the glyph the status rule requires. Eleven empty-state idioms, four table styles, two segmented controls written twice, six notification mechanisms with different lifetimes, and no context menu anywhere, including text fields. Every view swap unmounts the view, so filters, selections and scroll positions are lost on every tab change.

## What must survive

The authors were asked what any redesign keeps. Their lists agree:

Terminals are pooled outside React and re-parented, never re-opened; nothing animates around a live terminal; a view swap involving a live PTY is instant.
The PTY owns its keystrokes, with ⌘. as the one sanctioned exception, and every global handler bails inside the terminal host and under an open modal.
Status is glyph plus word before colour on every mark, and unknown statuses render as themselves.
The estimate grammar: tildes and "est." on every derived figure, "no measurements yet" rather than a false zero, confidence as a rule-derived value with reason codes.
Four read states everywhere: loading, failed, observed-empty, zero-in-window. Loading is not empty; a failed read is not an empty workspace.
The attention rank as Fleet's default order and the tiebreak for spend and age; the strip renders main's ranking untouched.
The composer fails closed and sends exactly the bytes a terminal would; the trust banner cannot be dismissed while a session runs above the default.
The palette is a real combobox with focus kept in the field and one complete index of every route.
The single-source semantic palette with its 4.5:1 contract; feature sheets declare no colour.
The shell toast is a message plus a runnable retry plus a route to the owning view.
Honest unsupported states rendered disabled with the reason in words, and consent and destruction handled in-page with counts.

## 1. How they handle it

T3 Code owns no memory and no skill content. Its durable state is an append-only event log plus one opaque provider resume cursor per thread (T3/apps/server/src/persistence/ProviderSessionRuntime.ts:36-51); continuity is Claude's session UUID or Codex thread/resume, gated by a computed continuation key (claude:home:<CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR>, codex:home:<shared home>) that both the picker and ProviderService.startSession enforce (T3/apps/server/src/provider/Layers/ProviderService.ts:1194-1214). Instruction files are left to each CLI; T3 injects one <runtime_info> line (T3/apps/server/src/provider/RuntimeInstructions.ts:2-13). Skills are a per-provider read-only catalog — filesystem scan where the CLI's loader is a directory walk (Claude, Antigravity), CLI/app-server RPC where config matters (Codex skills/list, grok inspect, OpenCode SDK) — surfaced as one $name token and translated at the adapter, verified against the CLI only for Claude and Cursor (T3/apps/server/src/provider/Drivers/ClaudeSkillDispatch.ts:1-78). Context is prompt text: invented <terminal_context>/<review_comment>/<assistant_citations> blocks under a 120,000-character cap, with a post-hoc context ring fed by provider usage events. What crosses providers is nothing; what crosses accounts is only what shares a config directory.

BridgeMind (BridgeMind One, successor to BridgeSpace) hosts the user's CLIs in panes and threads. Verified from the mirror: per-agent memory notes and skills behind one gear (v0.1.13), an agent session_search over its own past chats "prose only, scoped to that agent, most recent first" (v0.1.13), memory and skills text "fenced in its briefing as data, never as instructions" (v0.1.22), Claude launched from the app reading only user-level settings so a checked-in .claude/settings.json cannot grant permissions (v0.1.22), and starter skills synced by copying into every detected CLI's skills directory (v0.1.11). Its facts file states "It is not a memory product" (main.d1i77vrr9z6nq8.amplifyapp.com/llms-full.txt), while marketing sells a cloud "BridgeMemory hub" behind the hosted BridgeMCP endpoint with shared task state across Cursor/Claude Code/Codex — unverified (403). The widely quoted /flush, /dream → MEMORY.md mechanism belongs to xAI's Grok Build, an engine BridgeMind hosts, not to BridgeMemory (unverified attribution; see §6). Open-source skills are agentskills.io SKILL.md packages with optional metadata.hermes/metadata.openclaw blocks, installed by cp -r (BM/BridgeSpeak/skills/bridgespeak/SKILL.md:23-35; BM/BridgeWard/README.md:49-81). Wanigan is the only one of the three with a canonical, provider-neutral knowledge record (knowledge_items/knowledge_versions, W/src/main/db.ts:662-697), fed by bounded hook signals and explicit teaching, delivered as a query-scoped, token-budgeted, citation-re-hashed briefing on argv or hook (W/src/main/learning/briefing.ts:123-217; W/src/main/sessions.ts:918-951), and projected into provider files only as hash-guarded, reversible managed blocks (W/src/main/learning/projections.ts:207-297).

## 3. Where Wanigan is ahead

A canonical record exists at all. Versions, hashed file evidence, uneditable machine rationale, 90-day TTL for derived items (W/src/main/learning/repository.ts:515-557; W/src/main/learning-service.ts:655-700). T3 has no knowledge layer; BridgeMind says it is not a memory product.
Recall is validated before injection. Citations re-hashed at launch, stale items quarantined, previews never mutate (W/src/main/learning/staleness.ts:44-121; briefing.ts:178-193). Neither competitor checks what it recalls.

Nothing lands in the user's repo or CLI config. Settings, MCP config and briefings ride argv/userData paths (W/src/main/sessions.ts:879-938); projections are hash-guarded with Undo. T3 writes hidden refs into .git (T3/apps/server/src/checkpointing/Utils.ts:4-10); BridgeMind copies skills into every CLI and BridgeSpace wrote hooks into user settings.
Machine-authored knowledge cannot skip review by construction — confidence capped at 0.85 under a 0.90 gate (W/src/main/learning-service.ts:641-653).
Launch context is predicted, not guessed after. CLAUDE.md chain in load order, the AGENTS.md-not-read callout, MEMORY.md's 200-line/25 KiB cut, winning settings layer (W/src/main/context/instructions.ts:910-984; memory.ts:482-537).
Estimate grammar is uniform (~ and est. on every derived figure; no percentage for an unrecognised model; W/src/renderer/src/App.tsx:1268-1274).
Skill authoring with content checks and body reads with token estimates; T3 reads frontmatter only.
Accounts are config directories, never credentials, applied after pack env so a manifest cannot redirect them (W/src/main/accounts.ts:353-369).

### How this was produced

Wanigan was built from the current branch (task-graphs-and-accounts at d843b53) and driven headlessly with Playwright against the real Electron binary in a throwaway user-data directory. Every view was screenshotted at 1440×900 in both themes, again after seeding one project, one Control docket and one Learning candidate through the real IPC surface, and again in a 960px window. No agent session was launched, so the populated terminal state was read from code rather than seen. The renderer console and page-error channels stayed empty throughout.

T3 Code was read from a shallow clone of pingdotgg/t3code at commit 2dca7a1e (2026-09-04), plus its docs/ tree and two independent reviews with product screenshots. BridgeMind's site and docs return HTTP 403 to non-browser clients; a headless Chrome pass captured the homepage, its live in-page HTML re-creation of the app in Agent, Code and Chat modes, the dated changelog (v0.1.0 through v0.1.28) and the Terms and Privacy pages. Where a BridgeMind claim rests only on marketing copy it is labelled unverified. The BridgeMind demo window is an HTML re-creation, not a screenshot of the shipping binary, and its browser pane shows grey wireframe blocks.

Thirteen reader and researcher agents mapped the renderer and swept primary sources (Apple HIG, WAI-ARIA APG, WCAG 2.2, NN/g, product documentation), six more read the three systems' context, memory and skills machinery, and six produced visual anatomies and capability inventories. Proposal authors, comparison lenses and independent critics then worked from those maps. The adversarial pass, three lenses per item (is the premise true in the code or screenshot; does it respect Wanigan's values and load-bearing decisions; would an operator actually feel the benefit), completed for Part 3 and for the first twenty causes and adoption items in Part 1. It stalled twice on a usage limit before reaching the rest of Part 1 and all of Part 2, so those items are labelled as unverified author or critic findings. Ideas that failed are listed with the reason rather than dropped. About 130 agent runs and 14.7 million subagent tokens were spent across four workflows and two resumptions.

Word counts of first-paint prose were taken from the screenshots with wc -w, excluding nav labels, headings, stat labels and button labels.
