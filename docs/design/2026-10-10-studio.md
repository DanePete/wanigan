# Studio: Wanigan 2's interface, the work first

Dane chose this direction on 10 October 2026 from three mocked directions (A Studio, B Calm, C Workbench), for the
whole app, before beta.5 ships: "beautiful, polished, modern, feature rich and AAA". The mock is the reference for
look and layout: A · Studio, light and dark (the design canvas he reviewed; its source is in the session scratchpad
as `mocks/project/Main.dc.html`, copied into `docs/design/studio-mock/` for agents to read).

## Why

Wanigan grew one careful feature at a time. Each was right on its own; together they crowd the work. On a 1440 px
window the live view gives the site about a quarter of the screen, behind two rows of tabs and tools, a panel that
runs a layers tree into developer detail, a status line carrying an error, a following note and eight key hints,
and toasts stacked over the dock. Every other view has the same accretion: explanation paragraphs on first paint,
controls that each found their own place, borders nested in borders.

## Principles

Each settles a disagreement; each rules something out.

1. **The work is the hero.** The page in Live, the board in Board, the transcript in a session, the diff in
   Changes. Chrome recedes: one bar of navigation, one of tools, panels that close.
2. **One place per job.** Navigation is the rail. The view is the project bar. Tools are the toolbar. The thing
   chosen is the inspector (right). Structure is the left panel. Working with agents and debugging is the dock
   (bottom, closed by default). What just happened is the status line. Nothing else hosts these.
3. **Quiet by default; colour is an event.** Neutral surfaces. Blue (water) marks the selection and the one primary
   action of a surface. Amber means something needs you, and nothing else. Red means failed or refused. Green
   means saved or verified. No decorative colour, no gradient washes, no tinted cards for emphasis.
4. **Words where they decide something.** No paragraph of explanation on first paint. A view's purpose is its
   content; explanation lives in empty states, tooltips, the ? sheet and disclosures. Refusals and failures keep
   every word they need (honesty beats tidiness).
5. **Depth from surfaces, not borders.** Panels sit on the app ground with one hairline; nothing nests a bordered
   box in a bordered box. Raised things (menus, the element's action bar, dialogs) use elevation, not outlines.
6. **One message at a time.** The status line says the latest outcome; notifications collect in a tray (the bell in
   the rail) instead of stacking over content. A toast appears only for something the owner must act on now, and
   never covers a control.
7. **Every control has a name, a key and a visible focus.** Keys go through the Mod-key chords (⌘ on macOS, Ctrl on
   Windows and Linux) and are published on the shortcut sheet; nothing is reachable by mouse alone.
8. **Built for anyone.** Cross-platform from the first line (macOS and Windows; Linux where Electron is): no
   Mac-only metaphors, controls or APIs. Both themes are first-class. Contrast meets WCAG AA everywhere (4.5:1 text,
   3:1 large text and UI parts). Reduced motion is honoured.

## Components (`components/ui.tsx` and the shared bits)

Rebuilt, not added beside the old ones: every view moves to them.

- **Button**: primary (water fill, white text: one per surface), plain (hairline), quiet (text only), danger
  (red, for destructive confirms only). Sizes s (28 px) and m (32 px). Icon-and-label or icon-only (`IconButton`,
  32 × 30, its name in `aria-label` and a tooltip with its key).
- **Segmented / Tabs**: two forms. Pill tabs (a sunk track, the selected segment raised) for switching views or
  modes; underline tabs (2 px water underline) for a panel's sections.
- **Toolbar and ToolGroup**: a row of IconButtons in a sunk track, `role="toolbar"`, arrow keys move between them,
  a pressed tool shows water fill.
- **Panel**: a titled surface with optional underline tabs, scrolling on its own, resizable where it borders the
  work, collapsible to nothing (its key toggles it).
- **Section**: a small 11 px semibold label and its content; never a bordered box.
- **Field, Select, Toggle**: 32 px, 8 px radius, hairline at rest, water ring on focus; labels are real `<label>`s.
- **Menu and Popover**: float elevation, 10 px radius, 32 px rows, keys shown right-aligned in `KeyCaps`.
- **Dialog**: sheet elevation, 12 px radius, title, content, footer with at most one primary.
- **StatusLine**: one message (icon + sentence + optional action), the context's tabs waiting at its end, a ?
  button for the keys. Replaces the hint row.
- **NotificationTray**: the bell in the rail; entries with their time and action; the status line and toasts
  hand over to it.
- **Badge / Count / Dot**: counts in sunk or water-soft pills; the amber dot only for "needs you".
- **ProjectTile**: the rail's project button (mono key, 40 px, water ring when open, amber dot when it needs you).
- **EmptyState**: a title, one sentence, one action; the place explanation lives.
- **KeyCaps**: platform-correct keys.

## The frame

- **Rail** (60 px, `shell/Rail.tsx`): Wanigan's mark; Needs you (amber count); Sessions (running count); a hairline;
  the projects as tiles; space; Search and commands (Mod K); notifications (the tray); Settings. Every item has its
  name as a tooltip and its key. Accounts, Skills, MCP servers and the rest of today's sidebar items move into
  Settings' sections or the command palette; nothing is lost, each is one step away.
- **Project bar** (48 px): the project's name (a menu: switch, settings, open in Finder/Explorer); the views as pill
  tabs: Board, Sessions (count), Changes, Code, Live; History, Decisions and Activity under ··· (and in the
  palette); on the right the agents' state ("2 agents working", Wanigan's mark tinted by presence, never yellow)
  and Pause.
- **Command palette** (Mod K, `dialogs/Palette.tsx`): every view, project, session, page of a site and command,
  with its key.
- **Status line** at the bottom of each view (see StatusLine).

## Live (the editor)

As mocked. Toolbar (50 px): environment, back, forward, reload, the address (opens Go to); the tools in a
ToolGroup: Select (V), Type on the page (T), Style (S), Move (M) | Snap, Record, Voice; the widths (phone, tablet,
desktop), the layer's eye (show the page without the layer), Edits (count; opens the Edits panel), Make real
(primary; its menu: make all real, verify all). Left panel (248 px, Structure): Layers, Add (the library), Pages
(Go to's list). The canvas: the page as a sheet on `--bg-canvas`, the chosen element outlined in water with its
name chip, and its **action bar** floating above it (Style, Move, Take out, Ask an agent; Retype is the T tool and
a double-click). While typing, the chip says "typing · Enter keeps · Esc puts back". Right panel (292 px,
Inspector): the element's breadcrumb, name and kind; underline tabs **Design** (Words, Style, Applies to, the
layer state with Make it real and Verify), **Made by** (files, hooks, timings, cache: what developers read today
under "Why it looks like this"), **Data** (fields and values). The Edits panel replaces the Inspector when opened
(same place, its own key). Compare draws in the canvas, as today. The dock (Notes, Problems, Request, DevTools,
Code) opens from the status line's tabs or its keys, beneath or beside the canvas, closed by default.

## Every other view

Each gets the same pass: the work as the hero on `--bg-canvas` or the ground, one toolbar, panels that close, no
first-paint explanation, StatusLine at the bottom, the new components only.

- **Board, List, card drawer, New card**: lanes on the canvas, cards as panels without borders-in-borders; the drawer
  is the right panel (tabs: Overview, Review, Branch, Looks); filters in the toolbar.
- **Sessions, a session, Chat, Terminal, History**: the transcript or terminal is the hero; the session's facts in
  a right panel that closes; History as a list with a reader panel.
- **Changes, git views, Code**: the diff or editor is the hero; the file list is the left panel; commit and push in
  the toolbar; the merge and resolve flows as dialogs or the right panel.
- **Decisions, Activity**: lists with a reader panel.
- **Needs you, Running**: the queue as the hero, each row's one action in place.
- **Settings** (with Accounts, Skills, MCP servers, Local models, Phone, Live view and the rest as its sections):
  a section list on the left, one section at a time on the right.
- **Dialogs, onboarding, empty, offline and crash states**: Dialog and EmptyState; one sentence, one action.
- **Phone**: out of scope beyond sharing the tokens.

## Proving it

- The existing sweeps (UI, editor, phone, UI regressions, live, Go to, fit) stay the test of record. Their selectors
  and expectations change with the views, deliberately and in the same commit, never by deleting a check without
  an equivalent.
- Screenshots of every swept state in both themes; the reviewer opens them, view by view.
- `scripts/ui-fit.mjs` at 1280, 1440 and 1920: nothing drawn over anything else, the page or the work taking the
  largest area.
- A contrast check over the token pairs used for text, in both themes.
- Keyboard: every control reachable, its name announced, its key on the sheet.
- An independent review after the build (fresh eyes, UX and accessibility), and its findings fixed before release.

## Phasing

1. **Foundation**: tokens, components, the frame (rail, project bar, palette, status line, notification tray).
2. **In parallel, on the foundation**: Live; Board and cards; Sessions, Chat and History; Changes, git, Code,
   Decisions and Activity; Settings, the moved sidebar items, Needs you, Running, dialogs and states.
3. **Integration**: the feature branches (DevTools in the dock, Snap and Record and Voice as tools, Paragraphs,
   Webform, WordPress template words) in their places; the full test; the review; fixes.
4. **Release** beta.5.
